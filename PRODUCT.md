# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary users are customer stakeholders, sales engineers, and energy operations decision makers viewing a desktop large-screen EMS/MPC demonstration. They use the page in demos, review meetings, and product validation conversations where they need to quickly understand whether the MPC model creates measurable operating value.

## Product Purpose

The dashboard demonstrates the advantage of the product model by comparing factory strategy data with MPC strategy data. Success means customers can immediately see where the model improves outcomes, especially in cumulative benefit, maximum demand, peak-valley arbitrage benefit, battery cycles, and strategy curves.

## Brand Personality

Professional, technical, trustworthy. The interface should feel like an industrial energy management product: calm, precise, data-rich, and credible rather than promotional or decorative.

## Anti-references

Do not make the page look like a marketing landing page, consumer app, or decorative sci-fi screen. Avoid layout restructuring, oversized hero treatment, excessive animation, flashy gradients, nested-card clutter, over-bright neon effects, beige/cream themes, and generic purple-blue AI dashboard styling. The current dark industrial dashboard style should remain recognizable.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the existing layout and information architecture; visual work may improve typography, color, borders, contrast, and polish, but must not rearrange sections or change the three-column structure.
2. Make model advantage legible first: MPC comparison values, status, and curves should read as the page's evidence, not decoration.
3. Keep the interface product-like and operational: restrained color, consistent components, familiar controls, and clear states.
4. Use visual emphasis sparingly so customers know what changed, what is live, and what is controlled.
5. Optimize for wide desktop demo readability; screenshots should remain clear without scrolling or explanation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Do not rely on color alone for status meaning. Preserve keyboard-accessible native controls where possible. Any motion should be subtle, state-driven, and safe for reduced-motion users.
